MARITIME COMMUNICATION PROCEDURES

Document ID: COM-001
Title: Vessel Communication Procedures

1. GENERAL COMMUNICATION

Clear and timely communication is essential for safe vessel operations.

Operational instructions should be communicated using clear and
unambiguous language.

Important instructions should be acknowledged by the receiving
person.

2. BRIDGE TO ENGINE ROOM

The bridge and engine room should maintain effective communication
during maneuvering, departure, arrival and other operations requiring
coordination.

Changes to propulsion requirements should be communicated clearly.

The engine room should report significant machinery problems to the
bridge.

3. EMERGENCY COMMUNICATION

During an emergency, communication should prioritize information
that affects personnel safety, vessel safety and environmental
protection.

The following information may be important:
- Nature of the emergency
- Location
- Immediate danger
- Personnel involved
- Actions already taken
- Assistance required

4. COMMUNICATION WITH CREW

The responsible officer should ensure that crew members understand
important safety instructions.

Instructions involving hazardous operations should be communicated
before the operation begins.

5. RADIO COMMUNICATION

Radio communications should follow applicable maritime communication
procedures and the vessel's approved communication plan.

Emergency and safety communications should be kept clear and
concise.

6. COMMUNICATION DURING MANEUVERING

During maneuvering, commands should be clear and should be
acknowledged when required.

The bridge team should ensure that critical information is understood
by the person responsible for carrying out the command.

7. LOSS OF COMMUNICATION

If communication with a critical station is lost, the responsible officer
should take appropriate action according to the vessel's procedures.

Alternative communication methods should be used when available.

8. DOCUMENTATION

Important operational communications and significant events should
be recorded when required by vessel procedures.

9. COMMUNICATION PRINCIPLE

When safety-critical information is communicated, the sender should
confirm that the receiver has understood the instruction.
