FIRE EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

Document ID: FIR-001
Title: Fire Detection and Emergency Response

1. FIRE DETECTION

Any crew member who discovers smoke, flames or signs of fire should
immediately raise the alarm according to the vessel's emergency
procedure.

The location and apparent nature of the fire should be communicated
to the responsible officer.

2. GENERAL RESPONSE

When a fire is discovered, personnel should prioritize the safety of
people and follow the vessel's emergency response plan.

The fire alarm should be activated when required.

The bridge should be informed of the situation.

The emergency team should respond according to the vessel's
muster and fire-fighting plan.

3. FIRE LOCATION

The fire location should be identified as accurately as possible.

The crew should determine whether the fire is located in:
- Accommodation
- Engine room
- Galley
- Cargo area
- Electrical equipment area
- Deck area

4. ENGINE ROOM FIRE

If a fire occurs in the engine room, the emergency response should
follow the vessel's approved engine-room fire procedure.

The bridge, engine room personnel and emergency team should
coordinate their actions.

Fuel sources and ventilation may need to be controlled according to
the vessel's approved procedure.

Fixed fire-extinguishing systems should only be operated according
to the vessel's approved system instructions and emergency plan.

5. GALLEY FIRE

Cooking equipment can create a significant fire risk.

If a galley fire occurs, the responsible crew member should raise the
alarm and follow the approved galley fire procedure.

Appropriate fire-fighting equipment should be used for the type of
fire.

6. ELECTRICAL FIRE

Electrical fires require appropriate procedures.

Personnel should avoid using unsuitable extinguishing agents on
energized electrical equipment.

Electrical equipment should be isolated when this can be done safely
and according to approved procedures.

7. MUSTER

Crew members should proceed to their assigned muster stations when
the alarm requires evacuation or muster.

Personnel should follow the vessel's muster list and instructions from
the responsible officers.

8. FIRE CONTROL

Fire-fighting teams should use appropriate protective equipment and
follow the vessel's fire-fighting plan.

The spread of fire and smoke should be monitored.

9. AFTER THE FIRE

The area should be monitored for re-ignition.

Damage should be assessed after the situation is safe.

The incident should be reported and documented according to vessel
procedures.

10. SAFETY PRINCIPLE

No crew member should take unnecessary personal risk while
responding to a fire.

The vessel's approved emergency plan takes priority over generic
guidance.
