MARITIME WEATHER PROCEDURES

Document ID: WEA-001
Title: Weather Monitoring and Heavy Weather Operations

1. WEATHER MONITORING

The bridge team should continuously monitor weather conditions
that may affect navigation and vessel safety.

Relevant information may include:
- Wind speed
- Wind direction
- Wave conditions
- Visibility
- Precipitation
- Atmospheric pressure
- Storm warnings

2. WEATHER FORECASTS

The responsible officer should review available weather forecasts
during voyage planning and when significant weather changes are
expected.

Weather information should be obtained from appropriate and
authorized sources.

3. REDUCED VISIBILITY

When visibility deteriorates, the bridge team should follow the
vessel's approved restricted-visibility procedures.

Navigation equipment should be monitored carefully.

The appropriate lookout and navigation arrangements should be
maintained.

4. HEAVY WEATHER

When heavy weather is expected, the captain should consider
appropriate preparations based on the vessel's approved procedures.

Possible preparations include:
- Securing loose equipment
- Checking deck access
- Reviewing the planned route
- Monitoring vessel stability
- Checking weather updates
- Informing relevant crew members

5. STRONG WINDS

Strong winds may affect vessel handling and maneuverability.

The bridge team should consider wind effects during navigation,
maneuvering and port operations.

6. HIGH WAVES

High waves can increase vessel motions and may create risks to
personnel and equipment.

Crew members should avoid unnecessary exposure to hazardous deck
areas during severe conditions.

7. STORM CONDITIONS

If severe weather threatens vessel safety, the captain should evaluate
available options according to the vessel's approved procedures.

These may include changing course, reducing speed or seeking
shelter when appropriate.

8. WEATHER REPORTING

Significant weather conditions affecting the vessel should be
communicated to relevant personnel.

Important weather-related decisions should be recorded when
required.

9. WEATHER PRINCIPLE

Weather information should be continuously evaluated because
conditions can change during a voyage.

The captain should use current, reliable weather information when
making operational decisions.
